Omni Describer User Guide

Giving a Voice to the Visual World with AI.

Important Notice: Omni Describer has transitioned to a web platform. The desktop app is now in maintenance mode. New features and improvements are focused on the web version at studio.binclusive.io. The web platform requires no installation, no API keys, and works on all devices including Mac and mobile. The desktop app will remain available for those who prefer it.

It all started with my love for movies. When I realized how many details in my favorite scenes were lost without good audio description, an idea sparked: "Well, couldn't AI make this easier for us?" I dreamed of a tool that wouldn't just generate descriptions but would also give full control to the user. After months of intense work, countless trials, and overcoming many technical hurdles, I developed Omni Describer as the product of that dream.

What's in a Name?

The "Omni" in the name comes from Latin, meaning "all" or "everything." I chose this name because I didn't want the tool to serve just one purpose. Yes, Omni Describer primarily aims to make media accessible for blind and visually impaired individuals by creating audio descriptions. However, its purpose is not limited to that.

This is also an exploration tool. A film critic, a student, an artist, or anyone curious about visual details can use features like "Scene Explorer" or "Ask More" to delve into the layers of a video like never before. Omni Describer is a window to see the world through the "eyes" of AI and understand it differently. In short, it is "a describer for everything, for everyone."

System Requirements

To get the best performance from Omni Describer, I recommend meeting the following minimum system requirements:

  • Operating System: Windows 10 or newer (64-bit).
  • Memory (RAM): At least 4 GB of RAM.
  • Storage: At least 500 MB of free disk space for the application and temporary files.
  • Internet Connection: An active internet connection is required to connect to AI services (Google Gemini, OpenAI) and download videos.
  • Screen Reader: For full accessibility, a screen reader like JAWS, NVDA, or Windows Narrator is recommended.

Getting Started: Setting Up Your API Keys

Omni Describer uses cloud-based AI services to analyze and voice descriptions. Therefore, you need to enter your own API keys before you can start.

  1. Open Settings: Go to the File menu and select Settings... (or press Ctrl + ,).
  2. AI Settings Tab:
    • Gemini API Key: This is mandatory for video analysis. Paste your key into the "Gemini API Key:" field. You can get a free API key from Google AI Studio.
    • OpenAI API Key (for TTS): This is required for high-quality text-to-speech. Paste your key into this field. You can still use the built-in Windows voices without this key, but OpenAI is recommended for the best results. You can get a key from the OpenAI Platform.
  3. Save: Click Apply or OK to save your settings. You're now ready to go!
Secure Storage: Your API keys are encrypted and stored securely on your computer in the application's settings file. They are never sent anywhere else except to connect directly to the respective AI services.

At the first start, if no Gemini key is set, the app offers to open the Google AI Studio page and the Settings. Paste the key, then press Test key next to the field: the app tells you whether the key works and how many models it can use.

Quick Start: Generating Your First Description

Let's get started! Just follow these simple steps:

  1. Choose a Video: Click a button like "Local Video File" on the main window or select your video source from the File menu.
  2. Select a Prompt (Optional): The dropdown menu lists pre-made instructions that guide the AI. For your first try, leaving it at "(No Preset Selected)" is a great starting point.
  3. Start Processing: The application will now begin analyzing your video. You can follow the progress in the "Status Log" at the bottom of the window. This may take a few minutes, depending on the length of the video.

When the process is complete, the Described Video Player will open automatically, and you can start enjoying your newly described video!

Main Features

Before describing, the app finds the moments without speech, even under music (Silero speech detection, downloaded once, about 2 MB, and run on your computer), and the shot changes, then asks the AI to place descriptions there. It announces the approximate number of tokens before sending the video (Escape cancels), and gives news at least every 25 seconds while it works.

The Described Video Player

This is your personal, described movie theater. As the video plays, your installed screen reader (like JAWS or NVDA) will read the generated audio descriptions aloud at the correct moments.

  • Playback Controls: Use the Play/Pause, Rewind/Forward buttons, or the seek slider to navigate the video.
  • Current Description Area: You can follow the text of the currently active description here.
  • Edit and Refine: Click the "Edit Descriptions..." button to open a powerful editor for full control over your project.
  • AI Token Usage: This area shows you how many AI "tokens" were used, helping you keep track of your API usage.

Settings > Audio Output, "Descriptions in the player": your screen reader (speech and braille, the default); the app voice chosen above, prepared in advance so the video is lowered or paused for exactly the right time; or braille only, where the video pauses after each description until you press Enter. "Also show descriptions in large text on the video" displays each description on the picture, with a size and colour of your choice, for low-vision viewers.

Editing Descriptions: The Power to Refine

The AI provides a fantastic starting point, but true quality comes from refinement. The "Edit Descriptions..." button in the player opens a powerful editor that gives you full control over every aspect of your project.

  • Select and Modify: The list at the top has one row per description: start, end, text, words per second, review status and level. Selecting a row loads it below and moves the video there.
  • Adjust Timestamps: Edit the Start and End fields (HH:MM:SS.mmm), or set them from the player position. When you change the start time, the end time automatically shifts to preserve the description's duration. All times are validated to ensure they are within the bounds of your video.
  • Rewrite Text: Freely edit the description text in the main text box to improve clarity, add detail, or correct inaccuracies.
  • Add New Descriptions: Click the "Add New..." button to open a simple dialog where you can create a brand new description from scratch, setting its time and text.
  • Delete Descriptions: Select a description and click the "Delete" button to permanently remove it.
  • Characters: The "Characters..." button (Alt+H) opens the character glossary the AI uses to keep names consistent. Add, edit or delete a character (Enter edits, Delete removes). Giving a character a new name also renames it in every description.
  • Shortcuts: F8 / Shift+F8: next / previous description to review. Ctrl+P: play or pause the video. Alt+Left / Alt+Right: move the video 1 second. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y in the list: undo / redo. Ctrl+[ / Ctrl+]: set the start / end to the player position. Ctrl+S: save. Times are typed as HH:MM:SS.mmm (MM:SS or seconds also work); moving to another description keeps your changes, and closing asks if some are not saved.
Save Your Work: Changes you make in the editor (adding, deleting, modifying) are instantly applied to your current session. When you are done, simply click the "Close" button to return to the player. Your updated list of descriptions will be used for playback and for any subsequent exports.

For professional review, each description has a status (to review or validated) and reviewer notes, saved with the project. After generation, an AI check also marks descriptions that are not in the present tense, that interpret feelings instead of describing what is seen, or that leave the fiction (actors' names, camera jargon); they appear with "Review" in the editor, and V in the player counts them. Changing the text clears these marks.

Managing Prompt Presets

Prompts are powerful instructions that determine what the AI focuses on. By changing the prompt, you can get descriptions in vastly different styles.

  • Selecting a Preset: Before processing a video, choose a preset from the dropdown menu on the main window.
  • Managing Presets: Go to File -> Manage Prompt Presets.... Here you can Add, Edit, or Delete your own custom prompts. This is perfect for saving instructions you use frequently.
  • Language-Specific: Your prompt presets are saved separately for each language you select in the Settings.

The presets exist in the ten interface languages. Export... saves your presets to a file to share them with other describers; Import... adds the presets of such a file (a preset with the same name is replaced).

Ask More About the Scene

Ever wonder what a character is holding or what a sign in the background says? This feature lets you ask anything that comes to mind about the scene.

  1. Pause the video at the moment you're curious about.
  2. Click the Ask More... button.
  3. Type your question in the "Your New Question:" field (e.g., "What color is the woman's hat?" or "What does the writing on the wall say?").
  4. Select how many seconds of video the AI should analyze around the current time.
  5. Click "Submit Question." The AI's answer will appear in the "Conversation History" area.

Scene Explorer

Scene Explorer tells you what is in the picture and where. Each object comes with a clock position: 12 o'clock is the top of the picture, 3 o'clock the right edge, 6 o'clock the bottom. It is an ordinary window, read by your screen reader and braille display.

  1. Pause the video on a scene you want to explore in detail.
  2. Click the Explore Scene... button, then click "Analyze Scene".

The Scene Explorer window has three parts; move between them with Tab:

  • Scene: a summary of the whole scene.
  • Objects: the objects with their clock position. Moving through the list plays a sound placed to the left or right according to where the object is (use stereo headphones).
  • Details: what the selected object looks like and what it is doing.
  • Escape: close the Scene Explorer.

Images, PDFs and Your Screen

File > Describe an Image or PDF (Ctrl+I) describes a photo, a screenshot or a PDF document: main subject first, people, colours and any visible text, read exactly. For a PDF: layout, pictures, charts and tables with their key values, then a summary by section. The result opens in a window you can read line by line or copy.

Describe My Screen (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+D) works from any application: a browser, a video call, a game. The main screen is captured, described and the answer is read by your screen reader. The capture is deleted afterwards.

Live Screen Description (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L, on or off) checks the screen every 3 seconds and describes it only when it has changed noticeably, saying just what is new. Each description uses your API quota: turn it off when you no longer need it.

Audio subtitles: in Settings > AI, "Translate dialogue spoken in another language" adds the translation of foreign-language lines (anime in the original version, foreign films), introduced by the speaker. Like on-screen text, they are announced by a short sound and read in the second voice in exports.

Private mode: in Settings > AI, "Images and screen described by" can use a local model through Ollama (ollama.com) instead of Gemini. Nothing leaves your computer and no quota is used, but the descriptions are less accurate, and PDF documents still need Gemini. Install Ollama, then run ollama pull gemma3 (or choose another vision model in Settings).

With several screens, the screen showing the active window is described. In live mode, the new description waits for a pause in what the computer is playing (up to 8 seconds) before it is spoken.

Command Line and Batch Mode

To describe many files without the interface, use the command line. Folders are processed file by file; images and PDFs get a text description. The same settings and API key as the app are used.

python -m audio_describer.cli film.mp4 episodes --lang fr --export vtt,srt,txt,project --out out

Options: --density low, medium or high keeps only the essential descriptions or all of them in the exports (the project keeps everything); --export also accepts dapt, docx, mkv, mp4, mp3 and m4a; --watch keeps running and describes every new file copied into the folders. The installed app has the same command line: OmniDescriber.exe --cli followed by the same options.

Evaluating an Audio Description

Press E in the player to open the questionnaire from the 2020 audio description guide of the French audiovisual regulator (CSA). The first tab is for listening without the picture, the second for checking with the picture. Rate each question from 1 to 5 and add comments; measured values (descriptions over dialogue, words per second, uncertain descriptions, on-screen text) are shown next to the questions they inform. There is no overall score: the guide considers an average meaningless. The answers are saved with the project, and Save Report writes a text file.

Exporting Your Work

Once you're happy with your descriptions, you can export them from the Player Window in different formats:

  • Export to .TXT: A simple text file with timestamps.
  • Export to .SRT: A standard subtitle file you can use in video players like VLC.
  • Export Audio (MP3): This is perhaps the most exciting feature. It voices your descriptions with the voice you selected in Settings (SAPI5 or OpenAI), mixes it with the original video audio, and automatically lowers the background sound during descriptions to create an MP3 file you can listen to anywhere.

Other exports: video with a separate audio description track in MP4, for most players and phones; the script in W3C TTML DAPT for professional audio description tools; a production script in Word (.docx) with timecodes, text, level, status and notes; and the full script, where the AI transcribes the dialogue and interleaves it with the descriptions, a text alternative for deafblind readers (WCAG 1.2.8).

Options of the video and audio exports: Only the descriptions of the current density exports what you hear in the player (key D); Extended description pauses the picture when a description is longer than its pause, instead of cutting it (the video is re-encoded and the original subtitles are not kept); spoken credit adds at the end a sentence saying that the audio description was generated by AI. Without the extended option, the end message lists the descriptions that were cut. The audio description track is named in the description language, the subtitles of the source are kept when possible, and with 5.1 sound the descriptions are placed on the centre channel. In the player, Shift + I saves the audio introduction as an MP3 to publish before the programme.

Local and free: in Settings, AI tab, Video descriptions can use Ollama (local, free, private): the video never leaves your computer (install Ollama, then a vision model such as ollama pull gemma3); it is slower and less accurate than Gemini. Translated dialogue is read with a woman's or a man's voice matching the speaker: in the Audio tab, keep the automatic Windows voice or choose a local Piper voice and press Download and listen (about 20 MB, once). In the editor, Ctrl+G changes the speaker's voice of a translated line.

A Deep Dive into Settings

The Settings window (Ctrl + ,) gives you fine-grained control over Omni Describer's behavior.

General Tab

  • Allow descriptions to interrupt current speech: When checked, a new description will start speaking immediately, even if the previous one hasn't finished. Uncheck this to let each description finish completely before the next one starts. Useful for very fast-paced scenes.

AI Settings Tab

  • Frame Rate for AI Analysis: Determines how many video frames per second are sent to the AI. A lower value (e.g., 5 FPS) can significantly reduce API costs and help avoid processing limits, but may cause the AI to miss very fast actions.
  • Enable Video Chunking: For long videos (over 10-15 minutes), the AI can sometimes run out of processing capacity. This feature automatically splits the video into smaller parts, analyzes them sequentially, and stitches the results together. It is highly recommended for long-form content.
  • Disable Safety Filters (Use with caution): This option may allow the AI to process content that it might normally flag as sensitive. However, this is not an absolute override. The output is still subject to the AI provider's core safety policies. Please remember that you are responsible for how you use this feature.

Audio Output Tab

  • Text-to-Speech Engine:
    • SAPI5 (Windows Built-in): Uses voices that come with Windows or that you have installed. It does not require an additional API key. The app can access both modern 64-bit voices and legacy 32-bit voices. If you have older, favorite voices from past systems, choosing the "SAPI5 (32-bit Voices)" option will allow you to use them.
    • OpenAI TTS (High Quality): Produces more natural and fluent-sounding voices. Using this option requires a paid OpenAI account and API key. You can create and manage custom voice presets for OpenAI.

Also in the Audio Output tab: the pronunciation dictionary, one rule per line, such as Hermione = her-my-oh-nee, used by the app voice, the exports and the editor's listen button (whole words, upper or lower case); and the audio description mode the player starts in (in the pauses, extended or on demand; M still changes it). In the AI tab, the density setting chooses the density the player opens with.

Tips and Tricks for the Best Results

Creating great audio descriptions is an art. While AI is an effective assistant, you'll get the best results when you guide it correctly.

The Power of Prompts: Your Director's Notes

The application has a set of core rules it teaches the AI (like not talking over dialogue). Think of the Prompt Preset area on the main screen as the place where you provide your director's notes for that specific video. A good note helps the AI focus on a particular style or detail.
Tip: The "Focus on Names" Prompt
In a video with many characters, you can create a custom prompt to prioritize name tracking: Your highest priority for this video is to identify and use the correct character names as soon as they are spoken.
Tip: The "Describe the Atmosphere" Prompt
For visually rich films, guide the AI to focus on the environment: Focus on describing the setting, atmosphere, and environmental details. Mention the lighting, colors, and the overall mood of the scene.

What to Avoid in Prompts

Avoid instructions that contradict the AI's core principles (describing only visuals, not speaking over dialogue). Giving it a flawed instruction can cause poor results.

  • Bad Prompt: "Tell me everything that happens." This is too vague and can cause the AI to describe unimportant details.
  • Bad Prompt: "Tell me what the characters are saying." This will cause it to violate the "visuals only" rule.

In short: Use prompts to guide the AI's focus, not to change the fundamental rules of good audio description.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: My API keys are not working. What should I do?
A: First, double-check that you have copied the entire key correctly. For OpenAI, ensure you have set up a payment method in your account, as their TTS service is not free. For Gemini, ensure the API is enabled in your Google Cloud project.

Q: The process failed with a "MAX_TOKENS" error. What does this mean?
A: This means your video is too long or visually complex for the AI to process in a single pass. This is a capacity limit, not a content error. The best solution is to go to Settings -> AI Settings and enable "Video Chunking". This will automatically split the video into smaller, more manageable parts for the AI.

Q: The AI failed to generate descriptions because of "Safety Filters". What can I do?
A: This is a known issue where the AI's safety system can be overly cautious. You have a few options to try, in order: 1) Go to Settings -> AI Settings and enable "Disable Safety Filters" (this often helps). 2) If that fails, try reducing the "Frame Rate for AI Analysis" to send less data to the AI. 3) As a last resort, you can use the "Gemini Model Override" setting and enter gemini-4-argon. This model is more powerful and may handle sensitive content better, but it is slower and may be more expensive if you are on a paid API tier.

Q: Some of my old voices are missing from the SAPI5 list. Where did they go?
A: Modern Windows systems are 64-bit, but many classic, beloved text-to-speech voices were 32-bit. In Settings under the "Audio Output" tab, you will see separate engine choices for "SAPI5 (64-bit)" and "SAPI5 (32-bit)". To access your older voices, simply select the 32-bit engine.

Q: Why does generating descriptions take so long?
A: The time depends on your video's length, your internet speed (for the upload to the AI), the selected frame rate, and the current load on the AI services. Using "Video Chunking" is highly recommended for long videos.

Keyboard Shortcuts

  • Ctrl + O: Open Local Video
  • Ctrl + U: Open from Direct URL
  • Ctrl + Y: Open from YouTube
  • Ctrl + P: Open Project (.omniad)
  • Ctrl + G: Reuse the characters of an earlier episode (series)
  • Ctrl + I: Describe an image or PDF
  • Ctrl + Alt + Shift + D: Describe my screen, from any application
  • Ctrl + Alt + Shift + L: Live screen description on or off
  • Ctrl + ,: Open Settings
  • Escape: Cancel the running generation
  • Ctrl + Shift + P: Repeat the last progress message
  • F1: View Help

In the video player

These keys work whatever control has the focus, except that the description and token text boxes keep their own reading keys, and the arrow keys keep adjusting the volume slider when it has the focus.

  • Space: Play / pause
  • Left / Right: Back / forward 5 seconds
  • Ctrl + Left / Right: Back / forward 30 seconds
  • Alt + Left / Right: Previous / next description (jumps to it and reads it)
  • Up / Down: Video volume
  • R: Repeat the last description
  • T: Announce the current time and total duration
  • A: Turn audio description on or off
  • D: Change description density: low (essential only), medium, high (all details). Applies instantly, no new AI request.
  • M: Audio description mode: in the pauses (the video keeps playing), extended (the video pauses when a description is too long for its pause, then resumes), or on demand (nothing is read automatically)
  • Enter (on-demand mode): pause and describe the current moment briefly; Shift + Enter: everything described in the last 15 seconds
  • Q: Ask More about the current moment
  • S: Explore the current scene
  • I: Audio introduction: main characters, their look and the places, without spoilers, to hear before the programme. Written by the AI the first time, then saved with the project.
  • V: Quality report: number of descriptions, share within their word budget, descriptions over sound or marked uncertain by the AI check, words per second, longest stretch without description.
  • W: What is on screen now, including visible text. The answer appears in the Ask More window, where you can keep asking follow-up questions: the AI remembers the conversation.
  • E: Evaluate this audio description with the CSA 2020 questionnaire; the answers are saved with the project and can be exported as a report.
  • Shift+Up / Shift+Down: Lower the video more or less while a description is spoken (0 to 24 dB).
  • ?: Same as F1: speak the list of player shortcuts.
  • Ctrl + S: Save the project (Ctrl + Shift + S: Save As)
  • F1: Speak this list of shortcuts

While a description is spoken, the video volume is lowered by 9 dB (never muted, adjustable in Settings > Audio Output) and raised back smoothly, as recommended by professional audio description guides. A short sound plays before a description that reads on-screen text. During playback the descriptions are read by your screen reader, at its own volume; in exports their level is set in Settings > Audio Output.

In the description editor, each entry shows its time, level and words per second; entries that are too long for their pause or that fall over dialogue start with "Review".

More: File > Recent Projects reopens one of the last projects; File > Import Descriptions from SRT or WebVTT opens an existing description script with its video, to listen to it, edit it or export it. In the player, L chooses the next audio track of the video (for example an existing audio description) and C the next subtitle track or none; in on-demand mode a soft sound says that an essential description is available. In the description editor, Ctrl + R plays the text with the export voice.
